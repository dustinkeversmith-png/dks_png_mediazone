The Mathematical Blueprint for a Sub-Megabyte Engine1. Distill Neural Trajectories into Analytic Curves (Splines)Instead of hand-crafting target frequencies, train a modern teacher model offline (or analyze real speech datasets) to extract multi-dimensional parameter paths:Fundamental pitch ($F_0$)Formant centers and bandwidths ($F_1, F_2, F_3, F_4$)Open quotient and glottal pulse tiltAspiration / frication noise envelopesFit those continuous paths with piecewise cubic Bézier curves or B-splines. A 1-second syllable transition that takes tens of thousands of neural weights can be expressed as 6 to 10 control points per formant. In memory, this is roughly 40 to 80 bytes per diphonic gesture.2. Physical Glottal Flow Instead of Pure Sine/Square OscillatorsOld synthesizers buzzed because they fed an impulse train into static bandpass filters. Natural human voicing relies on continuous, non-linear pressure dynamics.Replace the oscillator with a parametric LF (Liljencrants–Fant) glottal flow model or a simplified two-mass vocal cord model.Compute excitation using simple DSP arithmetic in real time:$$U(t) = E_0 e^{\alpha t} \sin(\omega_0 t) \quad \text{during the open phase}$$This single change removes the harsh "8-bit buzz" and injects natural vocal fry, breathiness, and organic spectral roll-off—all running in standard C arithmetic with zero stored audio samples.3.
