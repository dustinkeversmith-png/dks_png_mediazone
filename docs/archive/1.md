### 2. Best & Fastest Way to Extract Formants and Power Spectrum

Formants represent the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract (the filter in the source-filter model).

* **Power Spectrum ($\vert{}X(f)\vert{}^2$):**
* Use a **Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)** computed via a vectorized FFT library (e.g., **FFTW3** or **PocketFFT**).
* Window the frame (Hamming/Hanning) $\rightarrow$ Execute FFT $\rightarrow$ Square the complex magnitudes.


* **Formant Extraction via Linear Predictive Coding (LPC):**
* Computing formants purely from an FFT peak picker is unreliable due to harmonic interference from the glottal source (Dirac comb).

* **The Gold Standard Pipeline (LPC Root Finding):**
1. **Pre-emphasis Filter:** Apply a first-order high-pass filter ($y[n] = x[n] - \alpha x[n-1]$, where $\alpha \approx 0.95 - 0.97$) to compensate for the $-6\text{ dB/octave}$ glottal roll-off.
2. **Autocorrelation & Levinson-Durbin Recursion:** Compute LPC coefficients ($a_k$) to approximate the vocal tract transfer function:

$$H(z) = \frac{1}{1 - \sum_{k=1}^{p} a_k z^{-k}}$$


3. **Polynomial Root Finding:** Find the complex roots $z_i$ of the LPC polynomial:

$$z_i = r_i e^{j \theta_i}$$


4. **Formant Frequencies ($F_i$) & Bandwidths ($B_i$):**

$$F_i = \frac{\theta_i \cdot f_s}{2\pi}, \quad B_i = -\frac{f_s}{\pi} \ln \vert{}r_i\vert{}$$


5. Discard poles with bandwidths that are too wide and sort by ascending frequency to identify $F_1, F_2, F_3$.




* **Fast C++ Tooling:**
* **[libpraat / Praat C++ bindings](https://github.com/praat/praat):** High-accuracy phonetic-grade Burg algorithm.
* **Custom Levinson-Durbin in C++:** Fast and straightforward to implement natively with **Eigen** for matrix/polynomial solving.

### 4. Source-Filter Physics in ASR: Source vs. Vocal Tract

```
[Glottal Source e[n]] ──► [Vocal Tract Filter H(z)] ──► [Speech Waveform s[n]]
  (Pitch / F0 / Dirac)         (Formants F1, F2 / LPC)        (Acoustic Output)

```

* **The Source (Impulse Train / Glottal Pulse):**
* Modeled as a periodic pulse train (fundamental frequency $F_0$) for voiced phonemes (vowels, nasals) or white noise for unvoiced fricatives (`/s/`, `/f/`).
* In the frequency domain, it generates the harmonic series (spectral comb).


* **The Filter (Formants):**
* The physical geometry of the pharynx, tongue position, and lips shapes the spectral envelope.
* Phoneme recognition predominantly relies on the **envelope peaks (formants $F_1, F_2, F_3$)**, which is why techniques like **Cepstral Analysis (Homomorphic Deconvolution)** and **LPC** were used historically to separate the glottal excitation from the formant envelope.
